    How this works

    Chances come from Gold Derby's combined odds (experts, editors and users). Nominated is Gold Derby's combined chance of a nomination. Win blends each group's win picks with the same weights Gold Derby uses for its nomination numbers. Percentages are rounded, and anything under 1% counts as zero and shows as a dash.
    Ranks everywhere are by nomination chance, with win chance breaking ties. An entry under the Cut below line (50% by default) is a long shot and is dropped. A film stays if it clears the line in at least one category.
    Cut below sets which contenders count: 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75% (locks), or Category limit, which keeps only as many films as a category nominates (10 for Best Picture, 5 elsewhere). A Lock tag means at least a 75% chance of a nomination somewhere (Song and Documentary don't count). On the Odds page, "Show long shots" adds contenders below the cut that still have at least a 2% chance. Any film in Best Picture's top 10 that has left theaters goes under Save for the Best Picture showcase and counts as planned unless you un-plan it.
    Ranked list: grouped by each film's highest category and sorted by Points inside each group, or as one list sorted by points or by most categories. Points add up a film's nomination chances (or win chances, if you switch it) across every category it clears the cut in. Table or Posters; seen films are faded and planned films are dimmed with a gray outline.
    Odds by category shows each category as a table, or as posters with a progress bar (performers' photos for the four acting categories). Biggest moves lists changes of 5+ points in nomination chance from the daily snapshots, since a month ago unless you pick another date, each with a small graph of its odds (the hollow dot is the date you compare with). Seen films in Best Picture's top 10 sit under Seen, back at the showcase. The second progress count leaves out films that aren't out yet.
    The site has three sections: Oscars (everything above), New movies and Diary. New movies shows films that opened in US theaters in the last two weeks and are still showing (opening dates from The Numbers, showtimes from JustWatch), films opening in the next week, movies that arrived on subscription streaming in the last three weeks (critic-reviewed ones only), and older films back in theaters, each with its poster, Rotten Tomatoes score, cast, aspect ratio, language, where to watch and the Wikipedia plot. Sort by popularity or Rotten Tomatoes (90%+ scores are highlighted). The search box searches these plus the Oscar contenders; "Ask Claude" covers anything else from Claude's own knowledge. "Want to watch" keeps a list at the top. A film's page also has its saved Plot from Wikipedia and the Full spoiler from The Movie Spoiler, folded up until you open them.
    Diary lists everything you've logged on Letterboxd (plus contenders marked watched here), newest first, dated by your Letterboxd diary (watch date and rating) or, for films marked on this page, the day you marked them. Once you've seen a film, its page becomes the after-the-movie page: the r/movies official discussion (a Reddit search, since Reddit blocks automated lookups), popular Letterboxd reviews (refreshed every few days), post-credits scenes from MediaStinger (with spoilers hidden until you tap), and links to dig deeper. It also holds your movies spreadsheet (1,332 theater trips from Dec 2011 to Dec 2024, with theater, screen, showtime, format, language, how you paid and notes) and your full Letterboxd diary from your data export (since Aug 2017, with ratings, tags and reviews). A spreadsheet row and its Letterboxd entry are one screening; where they disagree is listed in diary_report.md. The search box matches titles, theaters, formats, languages, directors, cast, genres, notes and dates; every word has to match. Tap a date for that whole day, laid out like a calendar day: each movie a block from its showtime, about 20 minutes of previews plus its runtime, with the breaks between. Tap a theater for everything you've seen there, and a movie for its details from Letterboxd (synopsis, director, cast) and every time you watched it. Stats groups charts by Over time, Days at the movies, Theaters, The movies and Paying for it, for any mix of years (tick them in the Years menu): movies per year, year by month, a week-by-week grid for a single year, month and weekday, movies in a day and marathon days, a 24-hour showtime clock, theaters (sortable; tap one) and theaters by year, premium formats ("LieMAX" and "Limax" count as IMAX), new vs. older films (two or more calendar years after release), languages, genres and how you paid. Tap a bar to see its movies. Shorts (under 40 minutes) are left out of the stats but stay in the log.
    Aspect ratio and language on each film's page come from Wikidata, which fills in over time; foreign-language films get a tag.
    Docs: documentaries are hidden everywhere except You Can See Everything and the Odds page, where they stay in their own category. Watching at home sorts the Watch page by where and when each film streams. Photos of performers come from Wikipedia (Wikimedia Commons). Rotten Tomatoes scores come through JustWatch, which shows Rotten Tomatoes' critics score.
    Where to watch is US only. Limited and wide release dates come from The Numbers' US release calendar (Wikidata as a fallback); theater listings and streaming from JustWatch. A film with a future US date counts as coming soon even if advance tickets are on sale. Weeks count from the wide release once it has happened, otherwise from the limited opening. "In theaters" means JustWatch lists showtimes, or the film opened in the last six weeks and isn't on home video. Weeks count from the first US theatrical date, limited or wide. Streaming means a subscription or free service; rent/buy only counts as a sign a film has left (or is leaving) theaters. Posters, cast, loglines and Rotten Tomatoes scores come from JustWatch; teasers from YouTube; plot summaries from The Movie Spoiler.
